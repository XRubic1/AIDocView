# 04 — Filing and notification

## How a claim typically gets filed

1. **Exception at delivery or post-delivery discovery**  
   Receiver notes on POD, refuses product, reports shortage/damage, or shipper later invoices the broker.

2. **Customer deducts or bills the broker**  
   Broker AP/claims records the deduction (example pattern: “Customer deducted $X”).

3. **Broker opens internal claim**  
   Assigns claim ID / load reference and gathers supporting docs.

4. **Formal notice to carrier**  
   Email/letter with claim notice, amount (if known), load #, and attachments (POD, photos, downloads, invoices).

5. **Insurance path (when used)**  
   Carrier or broker tenders to cargo insurance; adjuster assigned; inspection window may start.

6. **Holds applied**  
   Specific load(s) or entire account frozen; checks may be stopped or voided.

## What “good” notification looks like

Carriers (and factoring on their behalf) expect timely notice including:

- Reason for the claim
- Claim amount (or clear statement that amount is pending)
- Load / PO numbers involved
- Whether the **entire account** or only specific loads are on hold
- Current status (open, with adjuster, finalized, awaiting deductible, awaiting remittance)
- Date the carrier was notified
- Supporting documentation (POD, reefer download, photos, claim notice, invoices)

It is considered **customary** that the carrier is informed promptly after the claim occurs — including when the account is frozen and when a finalized dollar amount is set.

## Evidence packages by claim type

| Claim type | Core evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Temp / reefer | POD with protest notes, reefer download, temp instructions, emails proving instructions sent |
| Securement / tarp | Rate con requirements, photos, receiver reports, weather notes |
| Damage / shortage | POD, photos, packing lists, OS&D reports |
| Collision | Police/accident info if any, formal claim letter, unit counts & unit cost, tow/storage invoices |
| Trailer + product | Repair estimates, product invoices, photos |

## Formal cargo claim notice elements (broker → carrier)

From formal broker filings observed in practice:

- Broker claim ID and load number
- Total claim amount and loss type
- Date and location of loss
- Required acknowledgment: carrier’s **internal insurance claim number** + **assigned adjuster name**
- Inspection request deadline (commonly ~10 business days)
- Supporting documentation attached

## Factoring discovery paths

Factoring often learns of a claim **before** receiving a clean claims package:

| Discovery path | Example signal |
| --- | --- |
| Broker AP | “Account frozen due to a claim” / check voided |
| Aging / missing from weekly pay report | Loads not paid with no clear AP reason |
| Short payment received | Partial remit; remainder attributed to claim |
| Carrier informs factoring | Carrier already working with broker/insurance |
| Claims desk reply | After standard intake email |

## Documentation hygiene

Always ask (and file):

- When was the carrier notified?
- From which email address was notice sent?
- To which carrier email(s) was it sent?
- Were supporting docs and the **final amount** included?
- Has the claim notice been updated if the freight applied to the claim changed?

Carriers frequently dispute claims on the basis that they **never received** supporting documents or the finalized amount — even when the broker believes notice was given.
