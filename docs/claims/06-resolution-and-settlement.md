# 06 — Resolution and settlement

## Resolution paths

### A. Offset against carrier payables
Broker deducts the claim from freight bills owed to the carrier. Used when open payables are large enough. If not, account stays on hold until cash or insurance closes the gap.

### B. Carrier pays the broker directly
Carrier (or factoring at carrier direction) remits the claim amount. Broker then releases remaining loads.

### C. Insurance settles cargo loss
Cargo policy covers accepted product loss. Carrier usually still owes the **deductible** to the broker. Both pieces often required before hold release.

### D. Hybrid (most complete insurance settlements)
1. Adjuster confirms cargo product loss math.
2. Loss-handling expenses (tow/storage/disposal) paid **to vendors** (not duplicated in cargo demand).
3. **Release and Settlement Agreement** issued and signed by broker.
4. Insurer pays cargo portion to the **payee named in the release** (often the broker entity).
5. Carrier pays deductible directly to broker.
6. Broker applies both → reconciles claim → releases holds.

## Deductibles

- Deductible is the carrier’s responsibility even when insurance accepts the loss.
- Brokers track deductible separately from insurer remittance.
- Carriers sometimes ask to **apply the deductible against open balance** (credit open loads). Brokers may allow this — ask explicitly and get load numbers in writing.
- Hold release language often: deductible applied **and** still waiting on insurer remittance.

## Release and Settlement Agreement

Typical sequence:

1. Adjuster sends release for cargo-only (or agreed) amount.
2. Broker legal/claims reviews and signs.
3. Adjuster requests **payee / remittance instructions** for the broker entity.
4. Insurer processes payment.
5. Adjuster shares **proof of payment**.
6. Broker confirms funds received and applied.

Until step 6, factoring should treat the claim as **not released**.

## Payment tracing (frequent bottleneck)

Even after “proof of payment,” funds may not show on the broker’s side. Causes include:

- Payee confusion (carrier expected payment to customer; POP shows broker DBA)
- Check vs ACH vs wire unknown
- Missing check number / ACH trace / wire confirmation
- Accounting lag or misapplied remittance

**Adjuster follow-up checklist to request:**

- Payment method
- Date issued
- Check # / ACH reference / wire confirmation
- Remittance details sufficient to trace
- Whether payment cleared or remains outstanding

Factoring/carrier should keep pressing until broker AP/claims confirms **receipt and application**, not only insurer POP.

## Amount finalization

Claim amounts evolve:

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| No estimate yet | Open claim; still gather facts (e.g. securement) |
| Estimated | Working number; may change |
| Finalized | Broker states balance owed; payouts blocked until resolved |
| Updated notice | Freight applied to claim clarified; new docs sent to carrier |

Always confirm whether the amount on the hold matches the latest notice.

## What “with the insurance adjuster” means

Status update from broker claims often stops at: *claim is still with the carrier insurance adjuster.* That usually means:

- Liability/amount not fully settled for payment, or
- Waiting on carrier/insurer action, or
- Waiting on deductible / docs / inspection

Push for: adjuster name, insurer claim #, last action date, and what is blocking release.

## Closure for factoring invoices

When settlement succeeds:

1. Broker removes hold.
2. Previously held loads should appear on pay schedules / checks.
3. Factoring reconciles any short-pays already posted.
4. Document claim closed date and release confirmation in the invoice notes.

When settlement fails or carrier abandons:

1. Factoring may short-close invoices tied to that broker.
2. Carrier may take a chargeback / loss on factored amounts.
3. Keep written broker statements that payment will not release until claim paid.
