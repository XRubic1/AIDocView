# 01 — Claims overview

## What a freight claim is

A **freight claim** (cargo claim / loss & damage claim) is a formal demand for money because cargo, equipment, or related costs were lost or damaged in connection with a load. In brokered trucking, the broker’s customer (shipper) typically deducts or demands payment from the broker; the broker then pursues the **carrier** (and often the carrier’s **cargo insurance**).

Claims are not the same as ordinary invoice disputes (rate, accessorials, paperwork). A claim usually:

1. Freezes or reduces money the broker would otherwise pay the carrier.
2. Can hold an **entire carrier account**, not only the load that caused the loss.
3. May take weeks or months while insurance and adjusters work.
4. Often involves **deductibles**, **settlement releases**, and **proof of payment** before holds are lifted.

## Financial chain (simplified)

```
Shipper / receiver reports loss
        ↓
Broker customer deducts or bills broker
        ↓
Broker opens claim vs carrier (and notifies insurance when applicable)
        ↓
Broker places hold / short-pay / voids checks on carrier payables
        ↓
Factoring / A/R partner sees unpaid or short-paid invoices
        ↓
Carrier (+ insurance / adjuster) investigates, negotiates, pays
        ↓
Broker confirms settlement → releases holds → pays remaining loads
```

## Lifecycle stages

| Stage | Typical signals |
| --- | --- |
| **Discovered** | POD notes (“under protest”, temp exceptions), accident, shortage, damage at delivery |
| **Notified** | Claim notice to carrier; sometimes factoring learns first via AP (“account frozen”) |
| **Documented** | POD, photos, reefer download, BOL, claim notice, invoices for salvage/tow |
| **Amount set** | Estimated → finalized; may change as freight applied to claim is clarified |
| **On hold** | Single load, multiple loads, or **entire account**; checks may be voided |
| **In insurance** | Adjuster assigned; inspection window; policy deductible owed by carrier |
| **Settlement** | Release signed; insurer pays cargo portion; carrier pays deductible |
| **Payment tracing** | Proof of payment exists but funds not applied — common delay |
| **Closed / released** | Claim paid or settled; broker removes hold; remaining invoices pay |
| **Unresolved aging** | Factoring may short-close invoices, charge back, or escalate legally |

## Who “owns” resolution

| Party | Primary job on a claim |
| --- | --- |
| **Broker claims desk** | File notice, collect docs, hold payables, confirm settlement |
| **Carrier** | Respond, open insurance claim, pay deductible, dispute with evidence |
| **Insurance / TPA adjuster** | Cover cargo (and sometimes loss-handling expenses), issue settlement |
| **Factoring / A/R** | Get status, keep carrier informed, chase broker for hold release, manage aging |
| **Broker AP** | Stop/void payments while claim open; release when claims says so |

**Practical rule from the field:** many brokers prefer to resolve details **directly with the carrier**, not with the factoring company. Factoring should still ask the standard intake questions (see [07 — Ops playbook](./07-ops-playbook.md)) and **CC the carrier** so the carrier can argue facts the factor cannot.

## Outcomes that unlock payment

Holds typically stay until one or more of these happen:

- Claim amount is **paid in full** (carrier, insurance, or offset against payables).
- **Deductible** is paid to the broker and insurer remittance is received/applied.
- Signed **Release and Settlement Agreement** is executed and funds clear.
- Parties agree the claim is withdrawn / reduced / not carrier responsibility (less common without strong docs).

Until then, brokers commonly say: **nothing will be paid out until this is resolved.**
