# 08 — Case patterns (learned from real threads)

Anonymized patterns distilled from broker–carrier–factoring correspondence. Use as training examples, not as legal conclusions about any party.

## Pattern A — Temp claim + account freeze + insufficient payables

**Shape:** Reefer load delivered; POD “under protest” / product temped high; customer deducts five-figure amount; broker freezes carrier account; open freight bills are less than the claim.

**What factoring must do:**

- Run standard intake questions
- Obtain POD + reefer download
- Ask when carrier was notified of freeze and finalized amount
- Ask whether paying the claim releases specific held loads
- Have carrier dispute on the facts (instructions given, emails, downloads)
- Expect broker to say little will pay out until balance owed is cleared

**Lesson:** Finalized amount + “no payout until resolved” can appear weeks after delivery exceptions. Document gaps (who got which email) are a primary dispute lever.

---

## Pattern B — Damage claim discovered via voided check

**Shape:** Factoring chases past-due invoices → AP says a check for Load B was voided because of a pending claim on Load A → Claims joins thread → states ~five-figure (or larger) claim and hold until settled and paid → later “still with carrier insurance adjuster.”

**Lesson:** Payment ops (voids) are an early warning system. Always ask Claims for reason, amount, hold scope, and insurance status immediately after a void.

---

## Pattern C — Entire account held for old large claim

**Shape:** Current loads unpaid; broker says all carrier loads on hold due to a claim on an older load; reason includes trailer + product damage; amount can be six figures; carrier and insurance were notified months earlier; status remains open/in process.

**Lesson:** Confirm load number and claim date carefully — holds can look “wrong” when the unpaid invoice is new but the claim load is old. Entire-account holds can persist long after other historical claims were settled.

---

## Pattern D — Tarp claim spread across loads + deductible stall

**Shape:** Claim because load not tarped; amount in low five figures; not all loads held initially but recovery will spread; insurance waiting on carrier to pay deductible; broker asks factoring for help getting carrier response; invoices approach 60 days; factoring threatens invoice closure; eventual short residual payment may force short-close.

**Lesson:** Deductible inaction by the carrier is enough to freeze cash even when insurance “did its steps.” Factoring deadlines to the carrier are necessary to avoid endless aging.

---

## Pattern E — Accident claim; carrier “aware” but unresolved

**Shape:** Broker says accident in transit (e.g. ran over object); load will not release until claim resolved; factoring notifies carrier; carrier confirms awareness and will check internal claims team.

**Lesson:** Acknowledgment ≠ progress. Keep a follow-up cadence until there is an adjuster, amount, and release plan.

---

## Pattern F — Full insurance settlement still blocked on remittance

**Shape:** Collision cargo claim with formal notice, inspection window, product loss math, and separate tow/storage/disposal. Adjuster:

- Pays tow vendor directly for loss-handling expenses
- Confirms cargo product loss under policy
- Issues Release and Settlement Agreement
- Notes carrier owes deductible to broker
- Submits insurer portion for payment and later shares proof of payment

Broker still cannot release holds because funds are not located/applied; payee on POP is the broker entity; parties argue about who was supposed to receive payment; adjuster must pull method, date, and trace references from the insurer.

**Lesson:** Settlement paperwork and POP are mid-process, not the finish line. Hold release needs **broker cash application**. Factoring should stay on the thread until release is confirmed.

---

## Pattern G — Open claim, amount unknown

**Shape:** Broker reports open claim (e.g. improperly secured cargo) but **no estimated amount** yet; factoring updates carrier and continues follow-up.

**Lesson:** Still open a claim ticket and notify the carrier. Amount-pending is a first-class status, not “no claim.”

---

## Cross-cutting lessons

1. **AP and Claims are different doors** — use both; AP finds the hold, Claims explains it.
2. **Carrier must drive factual disputes** — factoring drives process, docs, and aging decisions.
3. **Hold scope changes money risk more than claim reason** — entire account > single load.
4. **Insurance progress ≠ payable release** — deductible + remittance application matter.
5. **Silence is expensive** — carrier or broker non-response pushes short-closes and chargebacks.
6. **Always re-verify identifiers** — wrong load # / wrong carrier assumptions happen when multiple invoices are chased at once.
