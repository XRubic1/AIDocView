# 03 — Claim types

Common claim reasons observed in broker–carrier practice. A single incident can combine more than one type (e.g. collision + product loss + tow/disposal).

## Temperature / reefer claims

**What happened:** Product arrived out of required temperature range.

**Typical evidence:**

- POD marked **“under protest”** with product listed as temped high/low
- Reefer download / continuous temp history
- Rate con / temp instructions given to the carrier
- Photos or receiver temp checks

**Disputes often turn on:**

- What temperature instructions the carrier was actually given
- Whether those instructions were sent, and to which email
- Whether the reefer download supports or contradicts the POD notes
- Whether the carrier was given supporting docs and the finalized amount in time

**Payment impact:** Customer deducts from broker → broker holds carrier account / payables until recovered.

## Improperly secured cargo

**What happened:** Load was not secured per requirements (straps, blocking, bracing, etc.).

**Typical status early on:** Broker reports an open claim but **may not yet have an estimated amount**. Factoring should still notify the carrier and track follow-ups.

## Failure to tarp

**What happened:** Load required tarping; carrier did not tarp; weather/damage results in a claim.

**Settlement pattern seen:** Insurance may complete its steps while waiting on the carrier to **pay the deductible**. Broker may hold multiple loads because no single payable equals the claim amount (“spread” the recovery).

## Freight damage (general)

**What happened:** Freight damaged in transit without a more specific public label yet (“damage to the freight”).

**Ops note:** Always push for a more specific cause, amount, and docs. “Damage” alone is not enough to advise the carrier on next steps.

## Collision / vehicle impact (cargo loss)

**What happened:** Accident or impact damages cargo (and sometimes equipment).

**Claim package may include:**

- Formal cargo loss claim notification with broker claim ID
- Date/state of loss
- Product loss calculation (units × unit cost)
- Separate tow, recovery, storage, disposal invoices
- Deadline to request inspection (e.g. within **10 business days** or inspection rights waived)

**Insurance split often seen:**

| Bucket | Who pays | Payee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product / cargo loss | Cargo insurer (minus deductible) | Often broker (named on release) |
| Tow / recovery / storage / disposal | Insurer as loss-handling expense | Often **tow vendor directly** |
| Policy deductible | Carrier | Broker |

## Carrier damage to trailer and product

**What happened:** Carrier allegedly damaged broker/customer equipment and product.

**Severity:** Amounts can be very large (six figures). Brokers may place the **entire carrier account** on hold for months, including unrelated later loads.

## Accident during transit (ran over object, etc.)

**What happened:** Driver incident during transit; broker refuses to release the load payment until claim is resolved.

**Ops pattern:** Factoring informs carrier; carrier confirms awareness and escalates to internal claims team. Keep following for status — “aware” is not “resolved.”

## Related but distinct: short-pay because of claim

Not a claim type itself, but a **payment outcome**: broker remits partial payment on a load (or closes it short) because claim dollars were deducted. Factoring may need carrier approval before closing the invoice short.
