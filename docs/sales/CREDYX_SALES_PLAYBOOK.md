## Part 1 — Straight talk on the first-draft pitch

I went through the whole product first: the portal, the Fee Schedule, the Carrier MSA, the marketing docs and the factoring comparison. Then I read the pitch. The instincts are right: lead with pain, position against factoring, make the fee feel safe. The execution has problems that will cost calls. Here is what works, what does not, and what to say instead.

### What works — keep it

- **"You pay only after you're paid."** The single strongest line we have. It removes the fear of paying for nothing, it is true (Fee Schedule §2), and no factoring company can say it the same way.
- **First month free on fees.** Concrete, easy to say, already on the website. Good.
- **Positioning against factoring.** Right instinct. Every carrier already understands factoring, so it is the fastest way to explain what we are. The argument itself needs to be fixed (below).
- **"We work with you."** The closing sentiment is right. Carriers are tired of vendors who treat them as an account number.

### What does not work — and what to say instead

### Three things missing from the pitch entirely

1. **Discovery.** The draft goes opener → pitch → price without learning anything. Five questions (trucks, who does billing, factor or not, brokers vs direct shippers, biggest headache) decide which pitch he gets. A two-truck owner-operator doing his own billing at 10pm and a twelve-truck fleet with a factor need different conversations.
2. **A next step.** The draft ends at "we work with you." Ends at what? Every call must end with one of: a booked 15-minute screen-share demo, permission to text the one-pager plus a set callback time, or a clean "no, don't call again." Anything else is not a result.
3. **The branches that happen on 70% of connects:** "I'm driving," "I have a factor," "how much." The script needs these ready before the first dial. They are in Section 3 and Section 6.

### Honest note on the hardest segment

A carrier who already factors gets paperwork checks, invoicing and collections from the factor, bundled into the factoring fee. Our extra value to him is real but narrower: visibility, someone chasing holds and short-pays for him, help with the brokers his factor declines, and a lower total cost if he ever moves off factoring. It is a harder sale and a smaller ticket (0.70%). Do not make it the main target. The main target is the carrier who does his own billing, or has a spouse or dispatcher doing it badly. Details in Section 2.

## Part 2 — The playbook

### 1. What we sell, in the carrier's words

Credyx is a back office for small trucking carriers. After a load delivers, the carrier sends us the rate confirmation, BOL and POD. We verify the paperwork, invoice the broker, follow up until the broker pays, and the carrier sees the status of every invoice in a portal on desktop or phone.

**We are not a factoring company.** We do not buy invoices, do not advance money, and never touch the freight payment. The broker pays the carrier directly (or the carrier's factor, if he has one). Credyx bills the carrier separately: a small percentage of what was actually collected.

**The five steps.** This is the only workflow we describe. Never say we confirm delivery or capture POD in the field.

| Step | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Docs received | Carrier sends the paperwork packet: rate con, BOL, POD, any accessorial backup |
| 2. Paperwork verified | We check it — signatures, matching load numbers, rates, missing pages — so it does not bounce |
| 3. Invoice submitted | We invoice the broker with a clean packet and track acceptance |
| 4. Broker follow-up | We follow up on due dates, work holds and disputes, keep the carrier informed |
| 5. Payment received | We confirm when the broker's payment lands — the goal is paid on time |

**Two plans**

| | Managed | Self-Service |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who does the work | Credyx A/R team processes packets, invoices brokers, follows up | Carrier uses the portal to submit and track; Credyx provides platform and support |
| Best for | Owner-operators and small fleets with no office person | Fleets with a bookkeeper who wants tooling and visibility |
| Fee | Same percentage schedule (Section 4) | Same percentage schedule |

Lead with **Managed** on cold calls. "We do it for you" is the product a carrier understands in one sentence.

**What the carrier gets that he cannot easily get elsewhere**

- Clean packets out fast. The biggest single reason small carriers get paid late is a sloppy or late invoice.
- A person following up on every broker, every due date, every hold.
- One place for every document: rate cons, BOLs, PODs, NOA, W-9, COI.
- Status on his phone: Submitted → Verified → Invoiced → Paid, with holds and disputes flagged.
- Paperwork review that catches missing pages, mismatched load numbers and wrong rates before we bill.
- No long-term contract: 30 days' notice to leave, no buyout, no NOA on his receivables.

**What we do not do.** Say it plainly if asked: dispatch, load booking, factoring or cash advances, compliance or IFTA filings, delivery confirmation. We do not guarantee that any broker pays by any date — we track and we chase.

### 2. Who to call

| Segment | Fit | Why | Difficulty |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Owner-operator, 1–3 trucks, no factor, does own billing | **Primary** | Bills at night, forgets accessorials, loses PODs, hates chasing brokers. Small fee in dollars, high pain. | Easy to reach, quick decision |
| Small fleet, 4–15 trucks, spouse or dispatcher bills, no factor | **Primary** | Billing person is overloaded, owner has no visibility, receivables are growing. | Best ticket size; needs a demo |
| Factored carrier who is tired of the fee | Secondary | Has cash reserves now, paying 2.5–3.5% on every load, wants out but fears the paperwork. | Good — we are the exit plan |
| Factored carrier, happy with it | Tertiary | Factor already invoices and collects. Sell visibility plus hold/dispute chasing at the reduced rate. | Hardest; smallest ticket |
| Dispatch services | Channel | One dispatcher serves 10–30 owner-operators and usually does not do billing. | Partner conversation, not a cold pitch |

**Where the lists come from**

- FMCSA carrier data (public): motor carriers with 1–15 power units. Target authorities **6–24 months old**. Brand-new authorities get 50 calls a day from factors, insurers and ELD vendors in their first month. Six months in, they have felt the paperwork pain and the phone has gone quiet.
- Referrals from every onboarded carrier. Ask on day 30.
- Trucking Facebook groups and owner-operator forums, for presence, not spam.

**When to call.** Tuesday to Thursday, 9–11am and 2–4pm in the carrier's time zone. Avoid Monday mornings (dispatch chaos) and Friday afternoons. Drivers do pick up while driving on hands-free; that is normal. Keep it short and ask for a callback time.

### 3. The call

The goal of a cold call is **not** to close. The goal is to earn a 15-minute screen-share demo, or permission to send the one-pager with a set callback time. Onboarding happens on the second conversation.

#### Opener — ten seconds

```
Hi, is this [Name]? This is [Caller] with Credyx. I'll be quick — I know you're probably driving.
We do the billing and paperwork for small carriers. Can I ask you one question, and if it's not for you, I'll let you go?
```

If "I'm driving" or "I'm busy": "No problem. When's a better time today or tomorrow? I'll call you then, two minutes max." Lock a time. Do not pitch a driver who says he is busy.

#### Discovery — the question that decides the pitch

```
Who handles your broker invoicing right now — you, somebody in the office, or a factoring company?
```

Then two or three of these, depending on the answer:

- How many trucks are you running?
- How long after delivery does the invoice usually go out?
- What happens when a broker sits on one? Who chases it?
- Mostly brokers, or direct shippers too?
- What's the most annoying part of the paperwork side?

Listen for: late invoices, lost PODs, unpaid detention or lumpers, a broker that is 60 days out, "my wife does it", "I do it Sunday night."

#### Pitch — Branch A: he does his own billing, or a family member does

```
Here's what we do. After you deliver, you send us the rate con, BOL and POD — snap a photo, email it, or upload it.
We check the paperwork, invoice the broker, and stay on them until they pay you.
You see every invoice on your phone — submitted, invoiced, paid, or on hold — so you're never guessing.
The broker pays you directly. We never touch your money and we don't buy your invoices. This isn't factoring.
```

#### Pitch — Branch B: he has a factoring company

```
Good — keep them if you need the money fast. We work alongside factors.
What they don't do is work for you. They check paperwork to protect their advance, and when a broker short-pays or disputes, it comes back to you as a chargeback.
We chase those for you, we handle the brokers your factor won't buy, and you see every invoice in one place.
Because your factor does the funding, our rate is lower — well under one percent.
And if you ever want to stop factoring, we're already running your back office, so leaving is easy.
```

Ask: "Does your factor ever decline a broker or a load? What do you do with those?" That is where the pain is for factored carriers.

#### Pitch — Branch C: a bookkeeper or dispatcher does it

```
Then you probably don't need to replace them — you need them to stop chasing brokers and re-sending PODs.
We take the invoicing and the follow-up off their plate, and you both see the status of every load in one portal.
```

Pivot to Self-Service if the bookkeeper is competent and just wants tooling. Managed if they are drowning.

#### Price — say it once, plainly, then stop talking

```
It's a percentage of what we collect — about one and a quarter percent for a fleet your size, charged after the broker pays you. Nothing upfront.
If a broker never pays and the invoice gets written off, there's no percentage on it.
Your first month, any invoice you submit has no fee at all.
```

If he has a factor on file: "With a factor on file it's zero point seven percent."

Do not say "best on the market." Do not add discounts. If he pushes, see Section 6.

#### Close — pick one, always

```
Two ways to go from here. I can do a 15-minute screen share and show you exactly what you'd see on your phone — or I text you a one-pager and call you back Thursday. Which is easier?
```

Then confirm: name, cell, email, best time, and how many trucks and who does billing today. That goes in the notes.

If it is a no: "Understood. Mind if I check back in a couple of months?" If he says do not call again, mark it and never call again.

### 4. Pricing — what to quote and why

**Quote the Fee Schedule. Do not improvise.**

| Carrier's monthly collected volume | Standard rate | With a factor on file |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under $100,000 | 1.25% | 0.70% |
| $100,000 – $250,000 | 1.00% | 0.60% |
| $250,000 – $500,000 | 0.90% | 0.50% |
| Over $500,000 | Agreed in writing | Agreed in writing |

- Applied to **confirmed collections** in the month — money that actually arrived.
- Pending, reversed and written-off amounts do not generate a percentage fee.
- Optional platform fee ($99 a month) only if agreed at enrollment. Recommendation for cold-call deals under 10 trucks: **do not add it.** It kills the "nothing upfront" line.
- Trial: first month, platform and collection fees waived on invoices submitted in that window.
- The rate tier is based on that month's collections. A growing fleet pays a lower rate as it grows. Say that.

**Why 0.8% / 0.4% is a mistake — the math**

A three-truck carrier grosses roughly $18–22k per truck per month; call it $60,000. That is 25–35 invoices a month, each needing verification, invoicing and one to three follow-ups.

| Rate | Monthly Credyx revenue on $60k | Per invoice (30 invoices) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.25% (schedule) | $750 | $25 |
| 0.80% (proposed) | $480 | $16 |
| 0.70% (schedule, factored) | $420 | $14 |
| 0.40% (proposed, factored) | $240 | $8 |

Eight dollars an invoice does not pay for a human to verify a packet, invoice it, chase a broker twice and handle a dispute. You would be buying customers at a loss and then trying to raise prices on them later, which is how you lose them. The schedule is already less than half of what factoring costs. Price is not the obstacle; trust and a clear next step are.

**What actually closes small carriers on price**

1. "After you're paid." Timing beats rate.
2. The free month. Try it with zero risk.
3. No lock-in. 30 days' notice, nothing to unwind.
4. Compared to what he pays now: a factor at 3% on $60k is $1,800 a month. We are $750. Say that number out loud.

**One rule:** the caller never invents a rate. If a carrier wants something off-schedule (big fleet, direct shippers only, unusual volume), the answer is "Let me have our team put a number together for you," and the founder decides.

### 5. Factoring — what the caller must actually know

You will be compared to factoring on every call. Know it better than the carrier does.

**How factoring works**

- The carrier sells the invoice to the factor. The factor advances 90–97% within 24–48 hours and holds the rest in reserve.
- The factor files a UCC-1 on the carrier's receivables and sends brokers a Notice of Assignment (NOA): pay the factor, not the carrier.
- When the broker pays, the factor releases the reserve minus the fee. Typical all-in cost for small carriers: **2.5%–3.5% of the invoice**, sometimes tiered by how long the broker takes to pay.
- **Recourse** (most common for small carriers): if the broker has not paid in 60–90 days, the factor charges the advance back to the carrier, from reserves or the next funding, plus fees. **Non-recourse** covers broker insolvency only; disputes, short-pays and paperwork problems still come back to the carrier. Both usually cost more than the headline rate suggests.
- Factors run credit on every broker. Brokers with slow pay history (45–60+ days), thin credit, a young authority, or a low bond get **declined ("no-buy")**. The carrier either turns down the load or hauls it and bills it himself, if his contract allows.
- Most factoring agreements include **exclusivity** (all invoices must be offered to the factor), **minimum monthly volume fees**, 12-month terms with auto-renewal, 30–90 days' notice to leave, early-termination fees, and a **buyout** if moving to another factor.

**The honest comparison**

| | Factoring | Credyx |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you are buying | Cash today — an advance | A back office — the service alone |
| Who owns the invoice | The factor (NOA, UCC) | The carrier |
| Who the work serves | The factor's receivable | The carrier |
| Money path | Broker pays factor; carrier gets advance minus fees and reserve | Broker pays carrier directly |
| Cost | 2.5–3.5% plus reserves, minimums, term fees | About 1% of collected; 0.7% with a factor; nothing upfront |
| Broker does not pay | Chargeback plus fees (recourse) | No advance to claw back; written off = no percentage |
| Declined brokers | Carrier is on his own | We invoice and follow up like any other load |
| Leaving | NOA release, buyout, notice period | 30 days' notice; nothing to unwind |

**Two things never to say about factors**

1. That they do not verify paperwork or do not do collections. They do, for their own invoice.
2. Any specific factor's name with a specific rate. Talk about factoring in general.

**Working alongside a factor — what "we work with your factor" really means**

- The carrier's NOA is stored in his Credyx vault and the account is flagged factoring-related (lower rate).
- We prepare and verify packets and route them the way the factor needs them; the broker still pays the factor under the NOA.
- We chase holds, disputes and short-pays that the factor would otherwise charge back.
- A factor's advance is **not** a Credyx collection event. We bill the reduced rate on confirmed collections.
- **Check exclusivity before promising to handle "the loads your factor won't buy."** Many contracts require every invoice to be offered to the factor first. Declined invoices can normally be billed directly, but the carrier should confirm with his contract.

**The alternative nobody mentions: broker quick-pay.** Many brokers pay in 1–7 days for a 1–3% fee. A carrier who only needs cash occasionally can use quick-pay on those loads and Credyx for everything. That is often cheaper than a factor on 100% of volume. Bring it up with carriers who are "thinking about leaving factoring."

### 6. Objections

**"I'm driving."** "Then I won't keep you. What time today or tomorrow works for a two-minute call?" Lock it.

**"I already have a factoring company."** "Keep them for the cash flow. What we add is someone working the invoices for you — holds, short-pays, disputes, the brokers they won't buy — and a portal where you see everything. Rate's lower because they fund. Does your factor ever decline a broker?"

**"My wife / my dispatcher does the billing."** "Then she's the one I'd love to save time for. Most of our clients had someone doing this at night. We take the invoicing and the chasing; she keeps the visibility. Would it be worth 15 minutes with both of you?"

**"How much?" (asked early)** Answer it. "About 1% of what we collect, charged after you're paid, nothing upfront, first month free. Want me to tell you how it works?" Never dodge price with a small carrier. It reads as hiding something.

**"That's too expensive / I do it myself for free."** "How many hours a week? At $60k a month we're about $750 — about $25 an invoice to have it verified, sent right away, and chased. Most people find they were leaving more than that in unbilled detention and late invoices." Then: "The first month has no fees. Run it side by side and see."

**"Why wouldn't I just factor instead?"** "If you need the cash in 24 hours on every load, factor. If you don't, you're paying 3% for financing you didn't need, plus reserves and chargebacks. We're 1.25% for the back office alone, after you're paid. Plenty of carriers use broker quick-pay for the odd load and us for everything."

**"Never heard of you. How long have you been around?"** "We're new — that's exactly why the first month is free and there's no long-term contract. Thirty days' notice, nothing to unwind. Try us on a month of invoices and judge us on that."

**"Do you take a cut of my payments?"** "No. The broker pays you directly. We send you a separate bill once a month for our fee, so you always see the full freight payment land in your account."

**"What if the broker doesn't pay?"** "We follow up, escalate, and help you gather what you'd need for a bond claim. We can't force a broker to pay — nobody can — but if it's written off, you don't pay us a percentage on it. We don't take a cut of money you never received."

**"Can you guarantee I get paid in 30 days?"** "No, and anyone who says yes is selling you a cash advance. What we control is the part that causes most late payments: a clean packet out fast and someone on the broker every step of the way. That's how you get paid on time."

**"Where are you based? / You have an accent."** Do not dodge and do not lead with it. "Our team is remote and our clients are US carriers. You'll have a US number and a named person to reach, and everything about your account is in the portal, in writing." Founder: give the caller a US number before he dials.

**"Send me something."** "Happy to. Can I text it to this number? And can I call you Thursday to answer questions — morning or afternoon?" A send without a callback is a no.

**"I'm too small."** "You're exactly who this is built for. Big fleets have an A/R department. You have a phone in a truck. We're the department."

**"I'll think about it."** "Sure. What's the one thing you'd want to see to be comfortable?" Answer it, then book the demo.

### 7. Never say / always do

**Never say**

- "Get paid faster," "same-day pay," "instant cash," "advance." These are cash promises and make us sound like a factor.
- "Guaranteed" anything about broker payment timing.
- "Best pricing on the market," "unbelievable offer."
- "Factors don't verify paperwork" or "factors don't do collections."
- "100% integration" with factoring companies.
- "We confirm delivery" or "we capture POD."
- Any rate that is not on the schedule.
- Features that are not live. The AI assistant is not a selling point on calls. Sell people plus the portal.

**Always do**

- Say "we are not a factoring company" on every first call.
- Say "charged after you're paid" every time you say a number.
- End with a booked demo, a booked callback, or a clean no.
- Log every call: trucks, who bills, factor yes/no, objection, next step.
- Ask permission before texting: "Can I text you the link?"
- Honor "don't call me again" permanently. Keep the do-not-call list.

> Compliance basics (not legal advice — confirm with counsel): manual, person-to-person dialing to businesses is standard practice. No auto-dialers, no pre-recorded messages, no unsolicited marketing texts — a cell phone in a truck is often a personal number. Identify yourself and the company on every call. Keep an internal do-not-call list and honor it.

### 8. Daily routine and the numbers

Cold-calling small carriers is a volume game with a low connect rate. Track it or you will fool yourself.

| Metric | Realistic target, first 60 days |
| --- | --- |
| Dials per day | 80–120 |
| Connects (spoke to a decision-maker) | 15–25% of dials |
| Real conversations (past the opener) | 30–40% of connects |
| Demos or callbacks booked | 10–15% of conversations |
| Onboarded | 20–30% of demos |

That is roughly **one onboarding per 100–150 dials** early on. It improves as the script tightens and referrals start.

**Follow-up cadence per lead.** Stop after touch 5 unless he engages.

1. Call, then text the one-pager the same hour if permitted.
2. Callback at the agreed time (day 2–3).
3. Text or email, day 7: "Still happy to do the 15-minute walkthrough — Tuesday or Thursday?"
4. Call, day 14.
5. Final text, day 30: "I'll leave you be — here's the link if the paperwork ever gets heavy."

**Notes template, one line per lead:** Company · MC · trucks · who bills · factor Y/N (which) · main pain · rate quoted · next step and date.

### 9. After the call — what to send and what onboarding needs

**Send within the hour:** the one-page overview and the demo link. Keep the message to three lines: what we do, first month free, the callback time you agreed.

**Demo, 15 minutes, screen share.** Show the invoice status stepper (Submitted → Verified → Invoiced → Paid), the document vault, the mobile view, and what a hold or dispute looks like. Demo data only. Never another client's real brokers or names.

**Onboarding documents the carrier will need.** Tell him on the demo so there are no surprises:

- W-9
- Certificate of insurance
- MC / DOT authority evidence
- Voided check or bank letter, so brokers pay him
- Notice of Assignment, if he factors
- Signed MSA with Exhibit A (Fee Schedule) and Exhibit B (Authorization to Act)

## Founder decisions needed before the first dial

These are yours to make, not the caller's. Each one is a gap between what the pitch says and what the contract or product says today.

1. **Pricing.** Confirm the caller quotes the Fee Schedule (1.25% / 0.70%), not 0.8% / 0.4%. If you want a small-fleet hook, my recommendation is the free month plus no platform fee, not a lower percentage.
2. **"No fee on uncollectable invoices."** Fee Schedule §5 lets Credyx add a fee on Managed force-close at 90+ days. Either remove that fee for launch clients or have the caller disclose it. Do not let the phone promise and the contract disagree.
3. **Turnaround commitment.** Carriers will ask "how fast do you invoice after I send docs?" Decide the answer (for example, within one business day of a complete packet) and put it in writing.
4. **Who runs the demo.** The caller books it; someone who knows the portal cold runs it, at least for the first twenty.
5. **Phone and email.** A US phone number and a credyx.net address for the caller. Test both before day one.
6. **The one-pager.** A single carrier-facing page: five steps, not factoring, price after you're paid, first month free, 30-day out, link. Same facts as this document, nothing more.
7. **Company location.** Decide the one-sentence answer to "where are you based" and make sure the caller uses it consistently.
8. **Lead tracking.** A spreadsheet is fine to start, with the columns from the notes template above. Review it with the caller weekly.
